## (R5) If You Change the Input Voltage, Will This Current Consumption Remain the Same?

**No, it will not remain exactly the same**, but the current consumption is relatively stable due to the built-in FET (Field Effect Transistor) impedance converter inside this electret condenser microphone (ECM). Here is the reasoning:

This microphone is an **Electret Condenser Microphone (ECM)** with an integrated FET impedance converter (as shown in the measurement circuit schematic in the datasheet). The FET acts as a **constant current source** in this circuit — it regulates the current draw so that, within a reasonable operating voltage range, the current remains relatively constant. This is unlike a purely resistive load, which would follow Ohm's law (I = V/R).

Context from the datasheet:
- **Operating voltage range**: 4.5 Vdc (typical) to 10 Vdc (max)
- **Current consumption**: max 0.5 mA at Vs = 4.5 Vdc
- **Sensitivity reduction (S-Vs)**: max -3 dB when Vs changes from 4.5 Vdc to 1.5 Vdc — this shows only a slight degradation in performance across that voltage range, further confirming the FET's regulating behavior.

Therefore, within the operating voltage range of 4.5V to 10V, the current consumption should remain relatively stable (near ~0.5 mA) due to the FET's constant-current characteristic — but it is not absolutely invariant; minor deviations may occur at the extremes of the voltage range.
